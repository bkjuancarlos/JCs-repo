Customer Experience · Standard 2

Engineering the Customer Experience

Lower the effort, for customers and for the agents who serve them

My original consulting framework — Engineering the Customer Experience — reduces customer effort through 7 core skills organized in four parts. Developed and facilitated to 400+ customer service and sales representatives across multiple organizations and markets, this model builds the mindset and behaviors that create effortless, memorable service interactions.

The framework: four parts, seven skills, one goal

Lower effort, for the customer and for the agent

Most disloyalty is created by effort, not by a lack of courtesy. The framework gives frontline teams and their leaders a shared, trainable way to guide every interaction so customers do less work and feel more in control, and so agents spend less energy negotiating and more time solving.

  • What I hear: 1. Active Listening · 2. Purposeful Small Talk. Capture what is said and uncover what is not.
  • How I respond: 3. Positive Language · 4. Advocacy · 5. Alternative Positioning. Frame solutions in the best possible light.
  • Develop self-awareness: 6. Understanding My Dominant Style. Know your strengths and your stress response.
  • Maintain self-awareness: 7. Flexing to Others' Personalities. Adapt so the skills hold up under pressure.

Why adopt it as your high-performing team standard

One standard, used at two levels. Agents use it with customers. Leaders use the same seven skills with their agents, because agents tend to treat customers the way they are treated. Certification is internal and practical: theory, role plays and observed live interactions, aligned with the COPC CX Standard R8.0 (2026).

🎯 For your operation

  • Effort predicts disloyalty. 96% of customers with a high-effort experience become more disloyal, versus 9% after a low-effort one.
  • Resolution pays back. Every 1-point gain in first-contact resolution lifts interaction NPS by about 1.4 points.
  • Humans now get the hardest contacts. Only 14% of service issues are fully solved in self-service, so the human conversation is where loyalty is won or lost.
  • Measurable, not motivational. Scorecards track CES, FCR, repeat contacts, transfers and critical errors before and after.

🧭 For your leadership team

  • Leaders design half the effort. The organization, not the agent, causes 49% of repeat calls: policy, tools and knowledge are leadership levers.
  • Managers set the tone. Global manager engagement is 22%, and managers account for about 70% of the variance in team engagement.
  • Coaching that sticks. A 5-step coaching conversation, quarterly calibration and monitoring rules aligned with COPC R8.0.
  • A culture, not a course. Daily effort huddles, a monthly "effort hunt" on repeat-contact drivers, and leaders certified on the same standard.

Sources: CEB/Gartner Customer Loyalty Survey (n=54,166), The Effortless Experience (2013); SQM Group FCR Benchmark (Feb 2025); Gartner (Aug 2024); Gallup State of the Global Workplace (2026); COPC CX Standard for Contact Centers R8.0 (Jan 2026).
